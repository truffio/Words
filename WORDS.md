# Words

*English Vocabulary Learning Game: Educational Concept*

---

## 1. Teaching Goal

Help a child who is starting an intensive English immersion program build practical English vocabulary. The game does this through playful, repeated exposure to familiar everyday places.

### Learning Objectives

- **Associate** spoken and written English words with recognizable objects.
- **Understand and recognize** common vocabulary in context, not as memorized isolated lists.
- **Build confidence**, listening comprehension, and word recall through short, enjoyable practice sessions.
- **Progress gradually** from recognizing a word to recalling and spelling it independently.

### Guiding Principle

The experience should feel like a **game**, not a conventional vocabulary test. Mistakes are chances to practice, never reasons for punishment.

---

## 2. Game Concept

The player explores **illustrated 2D scenes** of familiar settings, for example:

- Kitchen
- Dining room
- Bedroom
- Bathroom
- Living room

Each scene contains everyday objects that make up a **predefined vocabulary set**.

> **Example: Kitchen.** Refrigerator, sink, stove, cupboard, chair, table.

- Objects sit naturally within a coherent, visually engaging scene.
- The player interacts directly with objects by **touching or clicking** them.
- Each room gives a recognizable context that connects English vocabulary to the real world.

---

## 3. Core Gameplay Loop

1. **Choose a setting.** The player picks or enters a scene (e.g. the kitchen).
2. **Receive a challenge.** The game presents a short vocabulary challenge, **written in English and clearly spoken aloud**, e.g. *"Find the refrigerator."*
3. **Answer.** The player finds the requested object in the scene and taps it.
4. **Get feedback.** The game responds immediately with friendly visual and/or audio feedback. It either confirms the correct answer or encourages another attempt.
5. **Next challenge.** A new challenge appears, drawn from that setting's vocabulary.
6. **End of round.** After a short round, the game shows a simple performance summary and offers another round or a different setting.

> The interaction must be **quick, clear, and satisfying**, with little interruption between challenges.

---

## 4. Learning Activities

| # | Activity | What the game does | What the player does | Skills practiced |
|---|----------|--------------------|----------------------|------------------|
| 1 | **Listen and find** *(principal activity)* | Speaks a word or short instruction and also shows it in writing | Taps the matching object in the scene | Listening, reading, visual recognition |
| 2 | **Look and identify** | Highlights an object and shows several possible English names | Selects the correct word | Object ↔ written word association |
| 3 | **Recall and spell** | Highlights an object **without** showing its name | Types the English name | Active recall, spelling |

### 4.1 Listen and Find

The initial and principal activity. It combines listening, reading, and visual recognition.

### 4.2 Look and Identify

Reinforces the connection between objects and their written vocabulary.

### 4.3 Recall and Spell

A more demanding activity, meant for when words have become familiar. It encourages active recall and correct spelling.

### 4.4 Future Activity: Speak the Word *(not part of the initial game)*

The game shows an object and invites the player to say its English name aloud. The game could recognize the response and eventually give pronunciation feedback.

---

## 5. Feedback and Motivation

### Immediate Feedback

- Every answer gets **immediate, encouraging** feedback.
- **Correct answers** should feel rewarding.
- **Incorrect answers** give another chance and help reinforce the link between the word and the object.

### In-Session Display

A simple on-screen display may show:

- Number of correct answers
- Number of challenges attempted
- An optional timer or elapsed time

### End-of-Round Summary

A brief summary that **celebrates progress** and **encourages replay**.

### Out of Scope for the Initial Game

- Long-term player profiles
- Saved history
- Rankings / leaderboards
- Elaborate reward systems

> The focus is on **practicing vocabulary**, not managing scores.

---

## 6. Progression and Variety

- Vocabulary is **organized by environment**. Each setting has its own collection of objects.
- The player can **revisit any room** as often as needed.
- Challenges come in **varying order** so practice stays fresh.
- Learning progresses naturally:
  1. Identifying spoken words (*Listen and find*)
  2. Choosing written words (*Look and identify*)
  3. Recalling and spelling (*Recall and spell*)
- **New settings** expand vocabulary while keeping the same familiar interactions.

---

## 7. Desired Experience

The game should be:

- **Welcoming** and **visually consistent**
- **Easy for a child to use independently**
- Built for **brief, repeatable** practice sessions
- Clear in its **pronunciation** and **instructions**
- **Immediately responsive**
- Low on **distractions**

---

## 8. Definition of Success

The child increasingly **recognizes, understands, and recalls** everyday English words, and **wants to keep practicing**.
